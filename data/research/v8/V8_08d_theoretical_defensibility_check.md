# V8.08d Final Theoretical Defensibility Check

## Scenario naming

The term **Optimistic** is potentially misleading if read as an absolute
prosperity forecast: its 2050 unemployment is 12.0%, GDP per capita is below
2025, and median living is also below 2025.

Recommended reporting terminology:

**Low deterioration (optimistic / least-bad) / Central deterioration / High
deterioration (pessimistic)**

The existing scenario codes remain unchanged for data compatibility. In prose,
describe Optimistic as **Low deterioration / least-bad**, not as a claim of
absolute prosperity.

## Unemployment and GDP-per-capita defensibility

| Scenario | 2050 combination | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Low deterioration | 12.0% unemployment / EUR36,500 GDP per capita | DEFENSIBLE |
| Central deterioration | 14.0% unemployment / EUR34,000 GDP per capita | DEFENSIBLE_BUT_STRONG |
| High deterioration | 17.8% unemployment / EUR30,000 GDP per capita | DEFENSIBLE_BUT_STRONG |

These combinations are not evaluated through percentage increases in
unemployment. The relevant comparison is the percentage-point change from the
2025 7.725% baseline: +4.275 pp, +6.275 pp, and +10.075 pp respectively.

GDP per capita need not move one-for-one with unemployment. GDP per capita can
remain comparatively resilient when labor-force participation, hours worked,
employment composition, productivity, capital intensity, and automation offset
some employment loss. Conversely, GDP per capita can weaken substantially as
ageing, lower participation, under-investment, capital deterioration, reduced
hours, productivity weakness, and fiscal drag accumulate even when the
unemployment rate rises more slowly.

GDP per worker and GDP per capita also differ: a smaller or older labor force
can produce a different GDP-per-capita outcome than the unemployment rate alone
would imply. The frozen paths therefore do not contain a theoretical
contradiction.

## Historical and international plausibility

The combinations are within plausible advanced-economy regimes when treated as
conditional scenarios:

- France's early-1990s unemployment persistence supports elevated unemployment
  without an immediate collapse in output.
- Spain and Greece establish severe upper-bound unemployment and output stress.
- Italy supports a prolonged stagnation regime with productivity and fiscal
  constraints.
- Japan demonstrates that ageing and stagnation do not mechanically require
  high unemployment.

The central and high-deterioration combinations are strong assumptions, but
they are not inconsistent. Foreign episodes are analogues for plausibility,
not mechanically transferred forecasts.

## Median living relative to GDP per capita

The final median/GDP-per-capita ratios remain defensible:

- Low deterioration: approximately 67.7%
- Central deterioration: approximately 68.5%
- High deterioration: approximately 69.3%

A mildly rising ratio in worse scenarios can occur because French transfers,
pensions, redistribution, progressive taxation, unemployment insurance, and
public services partially protect the median household from aggregate output
losses. This protection is not unlimited: fiscal consolidation, tax pressure,
benefit constraints, wage weakness, inflation, housing costs, energy costs, and
long-term unemployment limit the effect over 25 years.

The earlier approximately 73% high-damage ratio was too strong because it
implied progressively greater long-run relative protection precisely where
fiscal capacity, employment, wages, and public-service support were most
strained. The revised ratio pattern retains cushioning without requiring an
unearned increase in protection.

## Exact methodological caveat

> FranceScope scenarios are conditional scenario estimates, not institutional
> forecasts. “Optimistic” means the low-deterioration or least-bad scenario
> relative to the other FranceScope scenarios; it does not mean absolute
> prosperity or an outcome better than 2025. Unemployment trajectories are
> scenario assumptions, not institutional forecasts. Real GDP per capita need
> not move one-for-one with unemployment because labor-force participation,
> ageing, productivity, automation, hours worked, employment composition, and
> GDP per worker affect the relationship. French social stabilizers can
> partially protect median living standards, although that protection is
> constrained over time by fiscal capacity, wages, inflation, housing and
> energy costs, and benefit pressure.
